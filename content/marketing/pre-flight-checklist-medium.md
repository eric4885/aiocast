## Article body (paste into Medium)

**Bottom line:** Most "bad podcast audio" is not mysterious — it is predictable. Run the same short checklist before every session so your files are edit-ready and transcript-friendly.

---

I have lost more episodes to preventable mistakes than to bad content: one guest on a shared track, clipping on the host mic, a window open during a heat pump cycle, a Riverside export that never synced before I closed the laptop.

None of that shows up in Spotify analytics. It shows up when you try to turn the episode into a blog post and the auto-transcript is garbage.

A **podcast preflight checklist** is the cheapest insurance you can buy.

---

## Why preflight matters if you care about SEO

Search traffic from podcasting usually starts on **your website** — a blog post or structured show notes — not inside Apple Podcasts.

Clean captures → cleaner auto-transcripts → less time fixing text before you draft an SEO article.

If you skip preflight, you are not just risking audio quality. You are risking the entire "podcast to blog" workflow downstream.

---

## Before you hit Record

**Room noise under control** — Close windows, move away from HVAC vents, record 10 seconds of silence. If you hear hum in headphones, fix the room before fixing it in post.

**One mic per voice** — Riverside, Squadcast, and local multitrack sessions should give you separate WAVs per speaker. One mixed track is harder to salvage.

**Gain staging** — Aim for speech peaks around -12 to -6 dBFS. Too hot clips; too quiet adds noise when you normalize.

**Closed-back headphones for guests** — Stops speaker bleed leaking back into the mic on remote calls.

---

## During the session

**Clap or slate at the top** — Sync scratch tracks when recording separate devices. Say the episode slug out loud if you batch multiple sessions.

**Watch meters** — Fix clipping and whisper-quiet dialogue before you wrap.

---

## After recording

**Back up to two locations** — Cloud + local disk before you edit.

**Listen through once before destructive cuts** — Map the story arc first. Structure beats filler removal.

**Remove filler after structure is set** — Lock sections, then polish delivery.
