## Article body (paste into Medium)

**Bottom line:** Show notes on *your website* should be a structured summary — hook, takeaways, topic seeds, one listener question — not a list of timestamps. That format feeds better blog drafts and FAQ blocks later.

---

Most indie podcasters treat show notes as an afterthought: paste the Apple Podcasts blurb, add three timestamps, publish.

Spotify and Apple rarely send meaningful search traffic to your site. **Google indexes text on your domain.** If your episode page only says "Episode 47 — 0:00 intro, 12:34 main topic," you are not giving search engines or skimmers a reason to land anywhere useful.

Good **podcast show notes** are a **template**, not a transcript dump.

---

## Why a template beats timestamps

**Cause chain:**

1. Thin episode pages → nothing unique for Google to rank.
2. No searchable question or outcome → no long-tail queries match your URL.
3. When you later try "podcast to blog," you are rewriting from scratch.
4. You skip publishing → the episode lives only in RSS.

A repeatable **show notes format** fixes step 1–3 before you hit record.

### What each field does

| Field | SEO / workflow use |
|--------|---------------------|
| **Hook** | Becomes meta description fodder (~155 chars when adapted) |
| **Takeaways** | Seed FAQ blocks and social hooks |
| **Topics covered** | H2 headings for a future blog post |
| **Listener question** | One primary keyword angle per episode |

## Mini filled example

```text
Episode title: Remote Podcast Recording on a Budget

Hook: Indie hosts can get interview-quality audio under $200 by fixing the room and gain staging before buying gear.

Takeaways:
- Treat the room before upgrading the mic
- Aim for -12 to -6 dBFS on speech
- Ask guests for separate tracks, not a mixed Zoom file

Topics covered:
- USB vs XLR on a budget
- Backup workflow before editing

Listener question: What is the cheapest way to get separate tracks for remote guests?
```
